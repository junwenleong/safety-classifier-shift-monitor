We present an online monitoring system for deployed safety classifiers that detects distributional shift, adversarial evasion and conformal coverage degradation. A pre-registered 800-cell factorial evaluation (4 classifiers x 5 shift types x 20 seeds x 2 windows) achieves an 86.6% control-validated detection rate (raw TPR 91.5%) with mean latency of 39.5 steps; a substantial classifier-by-shift interaction ($\eta^2 = 0.185$) precludes one-size-fits-all monitoring.
 The centrepiece is a score-disagreement canary: any un-targeted classifier detects when the primary is under gradient-based attack. We derive the security boundary, a confidence-gated stall condition at which a monitor-aware attacker's progress halts (gap $= 1/(2\lambda)$, observed/predicted ratios 0.86-1.00 at $\lambda \geq 2$), and give a 4-tier threat model with bounds across 35 frontier models (at least 71% detection for the best-value model, under 1.5% FPR across 10 models at N=1000). The defence is not absolute: 18.4% of adversarial inputs evade both primary and canary, the blocked rate saturates near 60%, and when inputs are not targeted at the primary, disagreement-based detection drops to 2% (1/50).
 Further results: weighted conformal prediction collapses silently for 3/4 classifiers through density-ratio failure in high-dimensional embeddings, and PCA to 32 dimensions restores coverage (+33pp on temporal shift); CUSUM/EWMA detectors catch persistent contamination down to 4% mixing; KS false-alarm rates inflate 5.4x under heterogeneous traffic while CUSUM is unaffected; and on o3, adversarial inputs need 3.3x more reasoning tokens than benign ones for valid scoring, a silent failure zone under standard budgets.
