We present an online monitoring system for deployed safety classifiers that detects distributional shift, adversarial evasion and conformal coverage degradation. A reduced 800-cell subset of a pre-registered factorial (4 classifiers x 5 shift types x 20 seeds x 2 windows) gives an 86.6% control-validated detection rate (raw TPR 91.5%), mean latency 39.5 steps and a classifier-by-shift interaction ($\eta^2 = 0.185$ complete-case; MNAR sensitivity 0.02-0.03) whose crossover motivates per-classifier monitoring. These values predate two detector-code fixes; regenerating all 800 cells with the corrected detector gives 86.8%, 39.8 steps and eta^2 = 0.186.
 The centrepiece is a score-disagreement canary: an un-targeted classifier detects when the primary is under gradient-based attack. We derive a stall condition at which a monitor-aware attacker's progress halts (gap $= 1/(2\lambda)$; observed/predicted 0.86-1.00 at $\lambda \geq 2$, n=20 per value) and a 4-tier threat model across 35 evaluated models. The defence is not absolute: the canary detects 75.5% (37/49) of primary-evading inputs, leaving a residual that evades both, and when inputs are not targeted at the primary, detection drops to 2% (1/50).
 Further results: weighted conformal prediction collapses silently for 3/4 classifiers through density-ratio failure in high-dimensional embeddings, and PCA to 32 dimensions restores coverage (+33pp on temporal shift); a no-shift control supports the dimensional explanation for 2 of 4 classifiers (decoders not run); KS false-alarm rates inflate 5.4x under heterogeneous traffic, while CUSUM's behaviour on that traffic is not established (two runs disagree); on o3, adversarial inputs follow a token-budget sigmoid shifted 2.5x higher than benign (T50 = 117 vs 46 tokens, 95% CI [104, 129]), and at a 16-token budget all three tested reasoning models return 0/100 valid scores, a silent failure zone.
